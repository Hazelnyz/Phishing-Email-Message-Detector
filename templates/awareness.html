Phishing Awareness

About the October 2026 reports: some students received messages claiming their VIT accounts would be suspended unless they clicked a link immediately. If you receive a message like this, do not click the link. Verify through the official VIT portal or helpdesk channels you already know and trust, not through any contact details given in the suspicious message itself.
Common Red Flags
  • Urgency: "act within 2 hours", "immediately", "final warning"
  • Threats: account suspension, deactivation, legal action
  • Credential requests: being asked to send a password, OTP, or PIN
  • Suspicious links: shortened URLs, lookalike domains, unusual top-level domains
  • Sender mismatch: an "official" message from a non-institutional email address
  • Unexpected money requests: fees, refunds, or scholarships tied to urgent payment
What To Do Instead
  • Pause before clicking — phishing relies on rushing you.
  • Go to the official site by typing the address yourself, not by clicking the link.
  • Verify with IT support or your institution through a known, trusted channel.
  • Never share your password or OTP with anyone, including "IT support" over chat/email.
  • Report suspicious messages to your organization's official security contact.
  • When unsure, use a tool like PhishGuard AI's Message Analysis page for a second opinion — but still verify independently.

How PhishGuard AI Reads a Message
  1. The message text is converted into TF-IDF features and scored by a trained classifier.
  2. The same text is scanned by rule-based detectors for urgency, threats, credential requests, suspicious URLs, sender mismatch, and payment requests.
  3. Both scores are combined into a single 0–100 risk score and mapped to HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW RISK, or INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION if the message is too short to judge.
  4. Every result includes an explanation and safe next steps — never just a bare label.
